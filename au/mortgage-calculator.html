How mortgage repayments are calculated

Mortgage repayments are calculated using your loan amount, interest rate, and loan term. Each repayment includes both interest and principal. Early in the loan, a larger portion of your payment goes toward interest, while later payments increasingly reduce the principal.

What affects your mortgage repayments?

  • Loan size – larger loans increase repayments
  • Interest rate – higher rates significantly increase total interest
  • Loan term – longer terms reduce monthly payments but increase total interest

Why extra repayments matter

Making additional repayments reduces your loan balance faster, which decreases the interest charged over time. Even small extra payments can save tens of thousands of dollars and shorten your loan by years.

Frequently asked questions

How are mortgage repayments calculated?

Repayments are based on a standard amortisation formula that spreads payments evenly over the loan term.

What happens if I make extra repayments?

Extra repayments reduce your balance faster, saving interest and shortening your loan duration.

How much interest will I pay?

Total interest depends on your loan amount, interest rate, and term. Longer loans and higher rates increase total interest significantly.

Is it better to pay weekly or monthly?

Paying more frequently can slightly reduce interest, especially if it results in extra repayments over time.

What is an amortisation schedule?

It’s a detailed breakdown of each payment showing how much goes toward interest and principal.

Can I pay off my mortgage early?

Yes, most loans allow extra repayments, though some fixed loans may have restrictions.
