How income tax works in Australia

Australia uses a progressive tax system, meaning your income is divided into brackets, and each bracket is taxed at a different rate. This means only the portion of your income within each bracket is taxed at that rate—not your entire income.

What is the Medicare levy?

Most Australian residents pay a Medicare levy of 2% of taxable income. This contributes to funding Australia's public healthcare system. Some low-income earners and specific groups may be eligible for reductions or exemptions.

What is HECS/HELP repayment?

If you have a HECS/HELP student loan, repayments are calculated as a percentage of your income once you earn above a minimum threshold. The repayment rate increases as your income increases.

Why your effective tax rate is lower than your top rate

Your effective tax rate is the total tax you pay divided by your total income. Because only part of your income is taxed at higher rates, your effective rate is always lower than your highest marginal tax rate.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax will I pay in Australia?

The amount of tax you pay depends on your income level, residency status, and any applicable deductions. This calculator provides an estimate based on current tax brackets.

What is the tax-free threshold?

Australian residents can earn up to $18,200 per year tax-free. Income above this threshold is taxed according to progressive rates.

Do non-residents pay more tax?

Yes, non-residents do not receive the tax-free threshold and are generally taxed at higher starting rates.

Do I always have to pay the Medicare levy?

Most residents do, but exemptions and reductions may apply based on income, age, or specific circumstances.

When do HECS repayments start?

Repayments begin once your income exceeds the minimum repayment threshold set by the Australian government.

Is this calculator accurate?
