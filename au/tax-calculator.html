How income tax works in Australia

Australia uses a progressive tax system, meaning your income is taxed in tiers. Each portion of your income falls into a different tax bracket, and only the amount within each bracket is taxed at that rate.

This means earning more money does not result in your entire income being taxed at a higher rate—only the additional income above each threshold is taxed more heavily.

Australian tax brackets (residents)

  • $0 – $18,200: 0%
  • $18,201 – $45,000: 16%
  • $45,001 – $135,000: 30%
  • $135,001 – $190,000: 37%
  • $190,000+: 45%

What is the Medicare levy?

Most Australian residents pay a Medicare levy of 2% to help fund the public healthcare system. Lower-income earners may pay a reduced rate or be exempt depending on income thresholds.

What is the Low Income Tax Offset (LITO)?

The Low Income Tax Offset reduces the amount of tax payable for lower and middle-income earners. It is automatically applied when you lodge your tax return and is included in this calculator for residents.

How HECS/HELP repayments work

If you have a student loan (HECS-HELP), repayments are calculated as a percentage of your income once you exceed a minimum threshold. The repayment rate increases as your income rises.

How to reduce your taxable income

Taxable income can be reduced through legitimate deductions such as:

  • Work-related expenses
  • Charitable donations
  • Investment-related costs
  • Superannuation contributions

Reducing taxable income can lower the total tax you pay.

Frequently asked questions

How much tax should I pay in Australia?

Your tax depends on your income, deductions, and residency status. Australia uses a progressive system, so higher income levels are taxed at higher rates.

Why is my effective tax rate lower than my marginal rate?

Your marginal rate applies only to the highest portion of your income, while your effective rate is the average across all tax brackets.

Do I have to pay the Medicare levy?

Most Australian residents pay a 2% Medicare levy. Some low-income earners may pay a reduced amount or be exempt.

When do HECS/HELP repayments start?

Repayments begin once your income exceeds a minimum threshold and increase as your income rises.

Can I reduce my tax legally?

Yes—through deductions such as work-related expenses, charitable donations, and super contributions.
